⚠ Fraud Threats & Defenses

Understand the most dangerous credit card fraud types and how to protect yourself

💳
Card Skimming
Criminals attach physical devices to ATMs or POS terminals to steal card data. The skimmer captures magnetic stripe data, enabling fraudsters to clone your card and make unauthorized purchases.
🎣
Phishing Attacks
Fraudsters impersonate banks or payment companies via fake emails, SMS (smishing), or calls (vishing) to trick you into revealing your card details, OTPs, and CVV numbers.
🌐
Online / CNP Fraud
Card-Not-Present fraud occurs when stolen card details are used for online purchases. With no physical card needed, fraudsters exploit data breaches or dark web purchases to shop online undetected.
👤
Identity Theft
Attackers use stolen personal information (PAN, Aadhaar, DOB) to open new credit accounts or take over existing ones, racking up debt in your name without your knowledge.
🤖
Account Takeover (ATO)
Hackers use credential stuffing, brute-force, or social engineering to gain access to your bank account, change contact details, and drain funds before you're alerted.
📦
Friendly Fraud
A customer makes a legitimate purchase and then falsely claims a chargeback, claiming the item was never received or was unauthorized — costing merchants billions annually.
🛡️ How to Protect Yourself
01
Enable transaction alerts via SMS/email for every card transaction, no matter how small.
02
Never share your OTP, CVV, or PIN with anyone — banks never ask for these over phone/email.
03
Use virtual cards or masked card numbers for online shopping on unfamiliar websites.
04
Regularly review your bank statements and dispute any unknown transactions immediately.
05
Enable international transactions lock if you don't travel abroad. Unlock only when needed.
06
Use two-factor authentication on all banking apps and keep your contact number updated.
07
Inspect ATMs for loose parts or unusual attachments before inserting your card.
08
Report fraud within 3 days to get full RBI-mandated zero-liability protection.
🏛️ Government Policies & Protections
⚖
RBI Zero Liability Policy: If you report unauthorized transactions within 3 working days, you bear zero liability — your bank must reverse the full amount under RBI's 2017 circular.
⚖
IT Act Section 66C & 66D: Identity theft and cheating by impersonation using computer resources is punishable with imprisonment up to 3 years and a fine up to ₹1 lakh.
⚖
Payment & Settlement Systems Act, 2007: Governs payment gateways and card networks, making them accountable for security lapses in the payment infrastructure.
⚖
NPCI Dispute Resolution: The National Payments Corporation of India provides a formal dispute mechanism for RuPay card fraud with defined SLAs for resolution.
⚖
DPDP Act 2023: India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act mandates data fiduciaries to notify users of breaches and imposes heavy penalties for negligence leading to financial harm.

⚡ Immediate Action Steps If You're a Victim
1
Block Your Card Immediately
Call your bank's 24×7 hotline or use the mobile app to block the compromised card within minutes. Most banks offer instant blocking via net banking too.
2
Call 1930 Within the Hour
Report to the National Cyber Crime Helpline immediately. Golden window for fund freeze is within 1–2 hours. Have your card number and transaction ID ready.
3
Register Online Complaint
File a detailed complaint at cybercrime.gov.in with screenshots, transaction IDs, and any suspicious messages received. You'll get a complaint number for tracking.
4
File Bank Dispute
Write a formal dispute letter to your bank within 3 working days to invoke RBI's zero-liability protection. Keep copies of all communications.
5
Follow Up & Escalate if Needed
If the bank doesn't resolve within 30 days, escalate to the RBI Banking Ombudsman at cms.rbi.org.in. For amounts above ₹1 lakh, consider filing an FIR at the cyber police station.
